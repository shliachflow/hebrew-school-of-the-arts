What the Morning Looks Like

Two and a half hours. Nothing wasted.

Parents always ask what actually happens. Here it is, start to finish.

  1. 10:00

    Arrival & Good Morning

    Coats off, aprons on. We start the day together with מודה אני and a look at the week ahead.

  2. 10:15

    Hebrew

    Small groups, by level, not by age — we teach with Aleph Champ, so every child moves at the pace that's right for them, from first letters to reading full tefillos.

  3. 10:50

    Kitchen or Studio

    Divisions rotate. Half the school is in the commercial kitchen; half is in the art studio. Next week they switch.

  4. 11:40

    Torah & Story

    The parsha, the holiday, the hero — brought to life by teachers who make you want to hear what happens next.

  5. 12:15

    Taste & Pickup

    We eat what we made. Whatever doesn't get eaten goes home in a container with your child's name on it.

Every division follows this rhythm, adapted to age. The little ones get more movement and more snack. The teen divisions get more depth, more responsibility, and more freedom in the kitchen.

Sprouts · Ages 3–5

Their first taste of everything.

The beginning of a Jewish life — and it should be delicious. Sprouts is built on songs, stories, textures, and the kind of learning that happens with your whole body. They'll know the Alef-Bet, the holidays, and the smell of a Friday kitchen before they can write their own name.

  • First Hebrew letters through song, movement, and play
  • Simple hands-on kitchen projects — mixing, rolling, decorating
  • Art, sensory stations, and story time
  • Holiday celebrations they'll never forget

Roots · Ages 6–8

Where it all starts to click.

The years the foundation gets built. Roots kids learn to read Hebrew, learn the story of our people, and spend real time in the kitchen and the studio — because a child who makes the holiday remembers the holiday.

  • Hebrew reading, taught in small leveled Aleph Champ groups
  • Full kitchen rotations — challah, latkes, hamantaschen, matzah
  • Art studio projects tied to the Jewish calendar
  • The stories, heroes, and holidays of a Jewish childhood

The Mitzvah Crew · Boys, Ages 9–13

Real fire. Real knives. Real responsibility.

The boys' division is built on something 11-year-old boys almost never get: actual trust. They run stations in a commercial kitchen. They take on real Torah learning. And they arrive at their Bar Mitzvah not with a memorized haftarah, but with an identity.

  • Full run of the commercial kitchen — real equipment, real recipes
  • Torah, Gemara basics, and the questions boys this age actually ask
  • Tefillin, mitzvos, and what Jewish manhood means
  • Bar Mitzvah preparation and one-on-one coaching

Her own program, her own space, her own mentors. The Bat Mitzvah Club is part Jewish learning, part kitchen, part art — and part something harder to put on a website: a room full of Jewish girls who are proud of who they are, guided by women they look up to.

Beyond the Classroom

A real commercial kitchen. Not a hot plate in a classroom.

We built it, we kashered it, and we handed it to the kids. Every division rotates through it, every single month. They measure, they knead, they burn a batch and make it again. And the Jewish calendar becomes something they can taste.

  • אלולChallah, braided six ways
  • כסלוLatkes and sufganiyot from scratch
  • אדרA hamantaschen production line
  • ניסןMatzah, 18 minutes, hands covered in flour
  • סיוןCheesecake, because Shavuos

A child who bakes the challah doesn't need to be told why Shabbos matters.

Everything is kosher, everything is supervised, and every allergy is accounted for. And yes — they bring it home.
